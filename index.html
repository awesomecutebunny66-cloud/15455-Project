Course project roadmap

Proposed website outline

The sections below are the planned order of the finished educational site. This page is currently a project outline, not the completed lessons.

01 / 08Motivation

Why quantum computation?

Introduce the goal of comparing classical and quantum computation. Motivate query complexity as a setting where we can rigorously study computational speedups.

02 / 08Foundations

Quantum computing essentials

Develop the minimum background needed for the algorithm: qubits, superposition, measurement, tensor products, and basic gates. Emphasize the Hadamard transform and interference.

03 / 08Framework

Circuits, oracles, and query complexity

Define how quantum circuits and oracle calls are represented. Explain what is counted by a query and why classical and quantum algorithms can be compared in this model.

04 / 08The problem

Simon's promise problem

State the hidden-string problem and its promise precisely, then work through a small example. Introduce a natural classical collision-finding strategy as a baseline.

05 / 08Algorithm

Simon's quantum algorithm

Walk through each stage of the quantum circuit, including the oracle and measurement steps. Show how repeated runs yield equations that can be solved using linear algebra over GF(2).

06 / 08Proof I

Correctness and quantum query bound

Prove why measured strings satisfy the required orthogonality relation with the hidden string. Analyze how many repetitions are sufficient to recover the hidden string with high probability.

07 / 08Proof II

The randomized classical lower bound

Develop the birthday-paradox intuition and a formal lower-bound argument for classical randomized queries. Combine this with the quantum upper bound to establish the exponential separation.

08 / 08Perspective

What the separation does—and does not—show

Compare the two query bounds and discuss the role of the oracle model. Briefly connect Simon's algorithm to later quantum algorithms and the broader study of computational complexity.

Possible additions: an interactive small-input simulation, a step-by-step circuit visualization, and a short optional section connecting the math to physical ideas about quantum systems.
